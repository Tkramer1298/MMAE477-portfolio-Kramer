precision-sizing

Reference only. Do not edit this file. Fill in sizing-table.csv and reflection.md in this same folder, those are yours. The sizing table opens directly in Excel, Numbers, or Google Sheets, no need to hand-edit it as text.

This builds on 00-drone-sizing-exercise. Don't re-research from scratch. Pull your own numbers forward from your first pass, keep your track (A or B) and your parts unless you have a real reason to change one, and tighten what's there. There is one new requirement: show that the motor you picked can actually lift the vehicle with some margin to spare.

Graded against the checklist below, not on completion alone. This pass is about precision, not exploration, that part already happened in 00.

What's different from 00

A few things are now fixed instead of open, so every student's numbers are built the same way and can actually be checked:

  • Vehicle configuration: a symmetric X-frame quadcopter, four motors, each carrying an equal share of the vehicle's weight in hover. This matters for the thrust check below.
  • Frame mass: fixed at exactly 100 g. Don't source it, don't range it, just use 100 g. It's still something you'll design later in the course.
  • Wiring, connectors, and power distribution: fixed at a 20 g allowance. This is genuinely hard to pin down precisely before a real layout exists, so it's given rather than researched. Don't spend time sourcing it.
  • The RC transmitter is ground equipment, not vehicle mass. It stays in the pilot's hands (or on Track A, it's the override link, still not onboard). List it if you like, but it does not count toward the vehicle's total mass. Only the receiver is onboard.
  • No propeller-set discounting games, no spec ranges. Every row needs a real, currently-sold part with a real link, the same rule as before, now actually checked.

Everything else about your build — which track, which specific motor, battery, flight controller, etc. — is still your call, exactly as it was in 00.

Step 1: tighten the mass and cost table

Open sizing-table.csv. It already has the fixed rows (frame, wiring) filled in, don't change those. For every other row:

  • One row per component type. If you need four of something, put the quantity in the Qty column, the mass of one in Unit mass (g), and let Total mass (g) be the product, computed, not guessed.
  • A real part, a real link, in the same row.
  • Mark whether it's Onboard (flies on the vehicle) or Ground (transmitter only). Only Onboard rows count toward the total.
  • Fill in the Total (onboard only) row at the bottom by summing the Total mass column for every Onboard row. This number is your vehicle weight, call it W (grams).

Step 2: check that your motor can actually hover with margin

This is the new part. Hover thrust isn't a design choice, it's determined by the weight you just computed: for a symmetric X-quad in hover, the four motors together produce exactly enough thrust to support the vehicle's weight, split evenly.

T_hover (per motor) = W / 4

(All of this is in grams-force, the convention the entire hobby motor industry already uses: a component's mass in grams is numerically the same as the weight it needs supported in grams-force. You don't need to convert to Newtons for this exercise.)

Now compare T_hover against what your motor can actually deliver. Real thrust numbers only exist as bench-tested tables (thrust in grams at a given throttle, prop, and battery voltage) — they are not something you can compute from a KV rating alone, and vendor listings almost never publish them. That's the research task for this step.

Pick a candidate motor

Choose one of these three (or keep your motor from 00, if you can find real documented thrust data for it — a source link is required either way). All three are the same 2207 stator size, 5" prop class, current production, and already close to what several 00 submissions picked:

  • T-Motor F60 Pro IV (2207.5, 1750/1950/2550KV, 4S-6S) — product page
  • iFlight XING2 2207 (1855/2755KV, 4S-6S) — product page
  • EMAX ECO II 2207 (1700/1900/2400KV, 3S-6S) — product page

None of these listings has thrust data on the page itself, that's normal for this industry, as you'll find out. Real bench-tested numbers for motors like these live in independent reviews, not on the vendor's page: written reviews with a data table, and video reviews that put numbers on screen while running the motor on a thrust stand, both count as a real source. Find one for whichever candidate you pick, and cite where the number came from — a name and a link, in your reflection.

The check

Using the max-thrust number you found, for the throttle/prop/voltage condition closest to your build, compute:

Margin = T_hover / (max thrust at full throttle)

Target: Margin ≤ 35%. That's not an arbitrary bar — it means your motor hovers well below its own maximum, leaving real headroom for maneuvering rather than running near its limit just to stay airborne.

Worked example, with made-up numbers, not a real motor's — say your tightened table comes out to W = 420 g, so T_hover = 420 / 4 = 105 g per motor. Say the review you found for your candidate reports 1500 g of max thrust at full throttle, at a cell count matching your battery. Margin = 105 / 1500 ≈ 7%, well under the 35% target. A build that light would actually be overpowered by that motor, which is its own useful thing to notice.

If your own numbers don't clear 35% with the motor you picked, that's a real result, not a failure: say so in your reflection and note what you'd change (a lower-KV motor, a bigger prop, a different candidate, or accept the reduced margin and explain why).

Step 3: reflection

Fill in reflection.md. It has the specific numbers this pass is looking for, don't skip sections.

One more thing

Add a row to ../DECISIONS.md for this pass: what you changed from 00 and why, with 01-precision-sizing/sizing-table.csv as the evidence link.

Grading checklist

Full credit needs all four:

  • Every onboard component is a real, linked part with correct Qty / Unit mass / Total mass, and the total sums correctly.
  • Frame (100 g) and wiring (20 g) are left at the given values; the transmitter is excluded from the onboard total.
  • The hover-margin calculation is present, correct, and cites a real, documented thrust source for whichever motor was checked.
  • reflection.md is filled in, DECISIONS.md has a new row for this pass, and the CSV is genuinely comma-delimited.

Partial credit is 50%, flat — for a real attempt that's missing one or more of the four items above, whether it's missing one or all three. 0% for 00's numbers resubmitted unchanged, or no meaningful attempt at the motor-margin check at all.
